  • A pollen tube grows out of the pollen grain on the stigma and carries at its tip the generative nucleus and the tube nucleus [4].

1. Explain briefly the following terms: (a) Ornithophily. Ornithophily is the pollination of flowers by birds [1]. Examples include flowers like Bignonia and Canna [1].

(b) Elephophily. Elephophily is the pollination facilitated by elephants [2]. It is found in Rafflesia whose flowers are very large and are found at ground level [2]. The pollen grains of one flower get stuck to the feet of elephants and may be carried to the stigma of another flower when the flower gets trampled by their feet [2].

(c) Artificial pollination. When man himself transfers pollen to the stigma, it is called artificial pollination [2]. It is a standard practice adopted by plant breeders and scientists to evolve new varieties [2].

3. What happens to the following after fertilization? (a) Ovules Ovules become the seeds [3].

(b) Calyx The calyx may either fall off or may remain intact in a dried and shrivelled form, such as in apple and guava [3]. In some cases, like brinjal, the green calyx persists and remains attached to the ripe fruit [3].

(c) Petals The petals wither and generally fall off [3].

(d) Stamens The stamens wither and generally fall off [3].

4. Mention any two contrivances in flowers which favour cross-pollination. Two contrivances that favour cross-pollination are:

  1. Unisexuality: The flowers may be either male or female and may be borne on separate plants, making cross-pollination the only possibility (e.g., palms and papaya) [4].
  2. Dichogamy: This is the different timings of maturation of the androecium and gynoecium [4]. The anthers and stigma of the same flower mature at different times, ensuring cross-pollination [4].
