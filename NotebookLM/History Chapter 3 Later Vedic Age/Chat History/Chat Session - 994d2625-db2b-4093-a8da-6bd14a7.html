**In the later Vedic Age there was a marked change in the economic life of the Aryans? Substantiate your claim with reference to (a) Agriculture, (b) Crafts, and (c) Trade and Commerce. **

Ans. There was a marked change in the economic life of the Aryans during the later Vedic Age, showing all-round development [1]. This claim can be substantiated as follows:

(a) Agriculture In the later Vedic Age, agriculture became much more important and productive than in the early Vedic period [2, 3]. While the early Aryans mainly produced barley (Yava), a new crop called Vrihi or rice assumed great significance during this time [3]. The later Vedic Aryans also produced many other crops such as wheat, millet, and sugarcane [3]. This growth was largely due to the use of iron axes, which made it easier to clear forests for cultivation, and iron ploughshares, which made deep ploughing easy [2, 4]. This increase in agricultural productivity eventually led to the growth of trade and the rise of towns and cities [2, 4].

(b) Crafts There was a remarkable variety of crafts in the later Vedic age because of the use of iron in large quantities [3]. Crafts such as leather-working, carpentry, jewellery making, and the manufacture of glass flourished [3]. A significant change was the development of craft specialization, where specific occupations became more common than in the early Vedic period [3]. For example, chariot-making developed into an occupation that was distinct from general carpentry [3]. Carpenters, masons, and metal workers also started working with better iron tools to develop different crafts [2].

(c) Trade and Commerce Important changes were seen in the field of trade and commerce, which became more organized and widespread [3]. Commerce was made easier by the use of units of value like nishka and satmana [3, 5]. References to ships in the texts indicate a flourishing inland and maritime trade [5]. Furthermore, many professions were organized into guilds, which controlled prices and ensured the quality of the goods produced [5]. Better transport facilities and the use of these units of value significantly contributed to the growth in trade during this period [4, 6].

B. Structured Questions

1. What information do we gather from the later Vedic literature in respect of:

(a) Spread of Vedic Civilization to many New Regions. Ans. The later Vedic literature shows that the Aryans moved along the course of the Ganga and Yamuna rivers [2]. The scene shifted from the Punjab to the land of the Kurus (modern Delhi), and they occupied vast regions to the east like Kosala, Kashi, Videha and Magadha [2]. The territories eventually comprised the whole of India to the north of the Narmada and some regions even to the south, like the land of the Andhras [2, 7].

(b) Role of Iron in later Vedic Economy. Ans. Iron axes made it easier to fell trees and clear forests, making more land available for cultivation [2]. Iron ploughshares allowed for deep ploughing, which helped peasants produce varied crops and increased agricultural productivity [2, 8]. This surplus led to the growth of trade and commerce, giving momentum to the rise of towns and cities [2, 8].

2. Describe the social changes evident in the later Vedic period with reference to:

(a) Position of Women Ans. In the later Vedic age, women were generally given a lower position and assumed a subordinate role [2, 6]. While they still took part in religious rites, the rules of marriage changed, and polygamy became the practice in higher sections of society [3]. The practice of widows marrying again was now regarded with disfavour [3]. However, some women still received a high order of education, as seen in the discourses of women philosophers in the Upanishads [3].

(b) Caste System Ans. The society was divided into four varnas, and membership of a caste became hereditary [3]. The Brahmins and Kshatriyas enjoyed powers and privileges denied to the Vaisyas and Shudras [3]. There was a fixation of occupations based on one's caste [3]. The position of the Shudras was made miserable, as they were deprived of the right to learn sacred texts and were forced to live in separate settlements as 'untouchables' [3, 8].

(c) Gurudakshina Ans. No fees were charged for education in the Gurukul system [4]. Instead, pupils paid a voluntary contribution known as Gurudakshina when they completed their education [4]. During their stay, children of both rich and poor families performed various duties like gathering fuel, tending cattle, and working in the fields attached to the Gurukula [4].

In the later period, many other crops like wheat, millet and sugarcane were also produced [4, 6]. The use of iron tools made agriculture more productive compared to the early period [2].

(b) Variety of Crafts Ans. The early Vedic age had crafts like making weapons, implements and ornaments [6]. In the later Vedic age, there was a remarkable variety of crafts such as leather-working, carpentry, jewellery making, and manufacture of glass [4]. Craft specialization became more common, and the use of iron in large quantities facilitated the development of these different crafts [2, 4].

(c) Changes in the field of Trade and Commerce Ans. Trade in the early period was mainly in cloth and leather goods [6]. In the later Vedic period, commerce was made easier by the use of units of value like nishka and satmana [5]. Many professions were organized into guilds which controlled prices and quality [5]. There are also references to ships, indicating a flourishing inland and maritime trade [5].
