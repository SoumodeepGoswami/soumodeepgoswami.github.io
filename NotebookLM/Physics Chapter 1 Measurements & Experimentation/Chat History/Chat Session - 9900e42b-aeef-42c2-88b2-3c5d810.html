Based on the sources, the factors affecting the time period of a simple pendulum are as follows:

  1. Effective length of the pendulum ($l$): The time period of oscillation is directly proportional to the square root of its effective length ($T \propto \sqrt{l}$) [1]. This means that if the length of the pendulum increases, its time period also increases [1]. For example, in summer, the pendulum of a clock expands (length increases), causing its time period to increase and the clock to go slow [1].

  2. Acceleration due to gravity ($g$): The time period of oscillation is inversely proportional to the square root of acceleration due to gravity ($T \propto 1/\sqrt{g}$) [1]. Because of this, a pendulum clock goes slow (its time period increases) when taken to mountains or mines where the value of $g$ decreases [1].

The relationship between these factors is given by the formula: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$ [2].

Additionally, the sources mention two factors that do not affect the time period:

  • Mass or material of the bob: Pendulums with different masses or materials but the same length will have the same time period [2].
  • Amplitude: The time period does not depend on the extent of the swing (amplitude), provided the swing is not too large [2].
